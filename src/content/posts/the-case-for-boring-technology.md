Every new project is a chance to try that shiny new framework. And every time, I have to talk myself out of it.

## Innovation tokens

Dan McKinley's "innovation tokens" metaphor is the best framing I've found. Every team has a limited budget for novelty. Spend it where it creates differentiation, not on commodity problems.

Your database doesn't need to be novel. Your deployment pipeline doesn't need to be cutting-edge. Save the innovation budget for the thing that makes your product unique.

## What boring buys you

Boring technology has documentation. It has Stack Overflow answers. It has known failure modes. When something breaks at 2 AM, you want a problem that thousands of people have already solved.

## When to break the rule

Sometimes the boring option genuinely doesn't fit. New problem domains, performance requirements that existing tools can't meet, or regulatory constraints that demand specific approaches. The key is making that choice deliberately, not by default.
